What are the drawbacks of impact windows?
Impact windows carry a higher upfront investment than standard windows, and their heavy laminated glass requires proper frame and structural support. Repairs can be costlier since a cracked pane often needs full replacement rather than a patch, and installation demands skilled professionals to avoid drafts or seal failures. What is the 25% rule for windows in Florida?
The 25% rule refers to a Florida building code trigger based on total glass surface area, not window count. If a project replaces more than a quarter of a home's glazed openings within a rolling 12 month period, every new window in that project must meet current impact resistant standards or use approved shutters, with permits and inspections required. Staying under that threshold still requires meeting basic code. LockTight Impact Windows can assess your home's glass area and coverage needs before you start planning.

What matters most is proper installation and certified products rated for your wind zone. Can I get tax credit for impact windows in Florida?
Availability of a tax credit or refund for impact windows in Florida varies and depends on current state legislation, your homestead status, property value, and the specific program's funding cycle at the time of purchase, so it's worth checking with the Florida Department of Revenue before assuming eligibility. Do impact windows lower homeowners insurance?
Often, yes. Impact windows can reduce the wind or hurricane portion of a homeowners insurance premium, since insurers view impact-rated glass as lowering storm damage risk. The actual savings vary depending on your insurer, location, and whether every opening in the home, including doors and skylights, is protected. A wind mitigation inspection is usually required to document the upgrade. LockTight Impact Windows can install code-compliant impact windows and provide documentation for your insurance review.
Can a burglar break impact windows?
Impact windows can crack under repeated heavy blows, but they are built to resist forced entry. The laminated glass holds together even when struck, so a burglar can't quickly punch through an opening. This buys valuable time and often discourages break-ins altogether.
